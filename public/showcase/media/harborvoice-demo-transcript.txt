A voice agent that sounds confident and is wrong is a liability. This is HarborVoice AI: a fictional port terminal, with an ElevenLabs voice agent called Nora, who answers shipment questions in four languages. Every record you will see is synthetic.

Nora can do exactly two things. She can look up a shipment by its exact reference, and she can ask a person to review it. That is all. There is no tool to release cargo, so no wording from a caller can make her release it. The model proposes. The server decides what is true.

Those rules live in code you can test without a model. No reference: ask for one. Unknown reference: say so, and invent nothing. Lookup system down: say it is down, and confirm nothing. A record that looks wrong: send it to a person. Eight tests. No API key. No credits.

Asking for a human is where many agents quietly mislead. Here, an escalation is a ticket marked pending review. Only an authenticated operator can approve or reject it, and only once. And approval does not change the shipment. The agent is told to say pending, never approved.

To check behaviour, write down what should happen before you look. AgentProof shows a deliberately unsafe policy failing thirty of a hundred and ten scenarios, and exactly which ones. Here, the lookup failed and the answer still claimed success. Switch to the corrected policy, and the same case recovers honestly.

AgentOps Kit does the same for configuration. Turn authentication off, or add a plain HTTP tool, and the check fails the build. Secrets are removed before anything is printed.

Be clear about what this shows. These are scripted fixtures. They prove the checks work. They do not prove that a language model is reliable, and they say nothing about audio. The few real provider tests are reported separately, and they are small.

The creative work is documented the same way. A narrated product film was rebuilt in ElevenCreative Flows after the first version lost the product on screen. The walkthrough shows the failure, the fix and the measurements, including what still goes wrong.

Everything is open source. Two tutorials and a ninety-minute workshop are free, and run offline. HarborVoice is an independent project, not affiliated with ElevenLabs. Try it, break it, and tell us what you find.
